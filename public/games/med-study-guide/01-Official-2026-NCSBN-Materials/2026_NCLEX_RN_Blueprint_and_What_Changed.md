## 1. Key Changes in the 2026 NCLEX-RN Test Plan

Unlike the major 2023 overhaul that launched the Next Generation NCLEX (NGN), the **2026 Test Plan is a clinical alignment and refinement update**:

1. **Client Needs Category Weights Are Unchanged:** All 4 overarching Client Needs categories and 8 subcategories keep the exact same percentage distributions as the 2023–2026 exam.
2. **Exam Format & Scoring Are Unchanged:**
   - **Length:** Minimum **85 questions** (70 scored + 15 unscored pretest items) up to a maximum of **150 questions**.
   - **Time Limit:** **5 hours** maximum.
   - **Case Studies:** Every candidate receives **3 unfolding NGN case studies** (6 questions each = 18 items) within the first 85 questions.
   - **Partial Credit Scoring:** Maintained (`+/-` scoring for SATA/Multiple Response, `0/1` for matrix/drop-down/highlight, and rationale dyads/triads).
3. **New Explicit Emphasis on Health Equity & Unbiased Care:**
   - Added explicit activity statements under *Management of Care*: **"Perform care to support unbiased treatment and equal access to care, regardless of culture, ethnicity, sexual orientation, gender identity, and/or gender expression."**
   - Stronger integration of **Social Determinants of Health (SDOH)** (housing instability, food insecurity, transportation barriers, health literacy) into clinical judgment case studies.
   - **Removal of Race/Ethnicity Labels in Stems:** Question stems avoid unnecessary race/ethnicity descriptors unless clinically relevant.
4. **Updated Clinical Safety & Monitoring Wording:**
   - Expanded language around evaluating internal and external fetal monitoring, maintaining client dignity and privacy across digital and physical settings, and managing substance withdrawal/harm reduction.

## 3. Next Generation NCLEX (NGN) 6-Step Unfolding Case Study Structure

Every 6-question unfolding case study follows the **NCSBN Clinical Judgment Measurement Model (NCJMM)** in exact order:

1. **Question 1 — Recognize Cues:** *"What matters most?"* Identify abnormal vital signs, high-priority lab values, or assessment red flags from the Nurses' Notes.
2. **Question 2 — Analyze Cues:** *"What could it mean?"* Link the client's cues to potential conditions or compare findings across two differential diagnoses (often a Matrix Multiple Choice or Multiple Response item).
3. **Question 3 — Prioritize Hypotheses:** *"Where do I start?"* Determine the most likely complication or highest-priority client problem.
4. **Question 4 — Generate Solutions:** *"What can I do?"* Identify anticipated provider prescriptions vs. contraindicated orders (often an Indicated / Contraindicated / Non-Essential Matrix Grid).
5. **Question 5 — Take Action:** *"What will I do?"* Perform the highest-priority nursing interventions safely (Select N, Drag-and-Drop, Cloze Drop-Down, or Bowtie).
6. **Question 6 — Evaluate Outcomes:** *"Did it help?"* Review updated Nurses' Notes or Flowsheets to determine which findings show improvement vs. clinical deterioration.
